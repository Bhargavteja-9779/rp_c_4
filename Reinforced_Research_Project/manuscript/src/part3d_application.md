
## 4.4. What this means for a harvest decision

To see what interval validity means in practice, we used the mango data for the task the handheld instruments are bought for: deciding whether fruit have reached the 14% dry matter recommended at harvest [@ainsworth2016]. About {{r_app_immature_share}} of the test fruit were below this threshold. Every test fruit came from a consignment that had supplied no standards, as it would in practice. A fruit was passed as mature if the lower limit of its 90% interval was at least 14%, and referred for re-measurement if the interval contained 14%. The usual practice, passing every fruit whose predicted value is at least 14%, served as reference (Table 5, Fig. 5).

The point rule passed {{r_app_point_imm_K10}} of the immature fruit after a season or instrument change (range over settings {{r_app_point_imm_K10_range}}). A single NIR value close to the threshold carries little information about which side of it the fruit is really on. Intervals carried over from the original calibration halved this ({{r_app_astm_imm_K10}}) but were still far too optimistic. With intervals rebuilt from the standards the rate dropped to a few per cent, and here the sampling of the standards mattered as much as the formula. With standards from two consignments, TJ+N still passed {{r_app_tjn_imm_K2}} of the immature fruit and Gaussian intervals {{r_app_gauss_imm_K2}}. With the same number of standards spread over ten consignments, the rates were {{r_app_tjn_imm_K10}} and {{r_app_gauss_imm_K10}}. The batch-level TJ+B was the most cautious ({{r_app_tjb_imm_K10}} with ten consignments). With only two consignments it referred every fruit, which is the honest outcome when the standards cannot say anything about new consignments.

The price of safety is the number of fruit that need a second look. With well-spread standards, TJ+N referred {{r_app_tjn_ref_K10}} of the fruit, and TJ+B {{r_app_tjb_ref_K10}}. This is not a weakness of the intervals. It reflects how close much of the fruit was to the threshold relative to the accuracy of a transferred handheld calibration (RMSEP {{r_app_rmsep}}% dry matter). An interval cannot make a measurement more precise. What it can do is tell the user which results can be acted on and which cannot, and it does so correctly only if it is valid for the consignments being tested.
