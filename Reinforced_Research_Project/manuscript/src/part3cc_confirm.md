
## 4.4. Confirmation in fourteen further instrument–season settings

The batch results above rest on four settings, and the planning model was formulated after seeing them. Both were therefore tested on data not analysed before: {{r15_n}} combinations of handheld unit and season ({{r15_units}} units), each with its own consignments, an original calibration built without any of their fruit, and slope/bias correction as the transfer. The predictions and pass criteria were written into the protocol and committed to the project's version history immediately before the runs (protocol, section E15); there is no external time stamp. The transfers worked well (RMSEP {{r15_rmsep}}% dry matter with 10 × 2 standards), and errors were again shared within consignments (intra-consignment correlation {{r15_icc}}).

The batch effect replicated without exception (Fig. 5a; Supplementary Table S15). With 20 standards from two consignments, the sample-level intervals were too narrow for new consignments in all fourteen settings: TJ+N in {{r15_tjn_K2}} (coverage {{r15_tjn_K2_cov}}), the Gaussian interval in {{r15_gauss_K2}} and the textbook regression interval of slope/bias correction in {{r15_ols_K2}}. With five consignments they still failed in {{r15_tjn_K5}} settings (coverage {{r15_tjn_K5_cov}}), and with ten in about half ({{r15_tjn_K10}} for TJ+N). With one fruit from each of 20 consignments, possible in two settings, none was too narrow. The ASTM interval of the original calibration was no better with two consignments (mean coverage {{r15_astm_K2_mean}}, too narrow in {{r15_astm_K2_under}}). The intervals that leave out whole consignments were never too narrow: TJ+B in {{r15_tjb_a01}} bounded cells at 90% and {{r15_tjb_a02}} at 80%, and the Gaussian and random-effects consignment formulas in {{r15_lgo_anova_under}} cells with five or more consignments at 90%. The random-effects interval was valid even with two consignments, but then {{r15_anova_width_k2}} times wider than TJ+N.

The planning model passed one of its two tests. In the first (P3), it was given each setting's intra-consignment correlation, estimated from the errors on the test consignments themselves, information a laboratory would not have in advance. Its predictions for the sample-level intervals then had a mean absolute error of {{r15_mae_tjn}} (TJ+N) and {{r15_mae_gauss}} (Gaussian), within the pre-set limit of 0.04 (Fig. 5b). Most of that agreement came from the larger designs. Within designs, the correlation between predicted and observed coverage was {{r15_r_K2}} with two consignments, {{r15_r_K5}} with five and {{r15_r_K10}} with ten, and the model was optimistic by {{r15_biasmean_K2}} on average with two consignments and by {{r15_biasmean_K10}} with ten. In the second test (P4), every setting was given the same prior correlation of 0.5. The predictions for five or more consignments were within {{r15_prior_maxdiff_k5}} of the observed mean coverage. For two consignments, however, the observed coverage fell below the prediction by more than the pre-set ±0.05, for both the Gaussian interval ({{r15_prior_k2_obs}} against {{r15_prior_k2_pred}}) and TJ+N ({{r15_prior_k2_tjn_obs}} against {{r15_prior_k2_tjn_pred}}). As specified in the protocol for this outcome, we treat the model as descriptive: it captures how the loss grows as the number of batches falls, and it should be read as an optimistic bound, not as a prediction a laboratory can rely on.
