Transfer Interval Tool

For NIR calibrations moved to a new instrument or season with slope/bias correction. Plan how to collect the reference samples, then turn the instrument's predictions into corrected values with prediction intervals that hold for future batches. Everything runs in your browser; no data leave the page.

1 · Plan the reference samples

Expected coverage of a prediction interval for a sample from a new batch, from a random-effects model of the transfer errors. The intra-batch correlation describes how strongly errors are shared within a lot; 0.5 is a typical value in the mango data behind this tool.

Samples per batch are taken as reference samples ÷ batches (rounded down). 6,000 simulated draws.

2 · Intervals from your reference samples

Paste the original calibration's predictions and the reference values of the transfer standards (with their batch if known), and the predictions for routine samples. The tool fits slope/bias correction and builds the interval by refitting it with each standard, or each batch, left out (transfer jackknife+).

Example data: simulated mango dry matter (%), 20 reference fruit from 10 consignments

#PredictionCorrectedLowerUpperDecision
Method: slope/bias correction refitted with each reference sample (or batch) left out; interval from the left-out predictions and errors (jackknife+; Barber et al., Ann. Stat. 2021; batch version: Lee, Barber & Willett, ACM J. Data Sci. 2026). Coverage holds on average if the reference samples, or their batches, are a random draw of what will be measured later, and if the instrument does not drift after set-up. A batch-level 90 % jackknife+ interval needs at least 9 batches. Decision: pass if the whole interval is at or above the threshold, fail if it is entirely below, otherwise re-measure.
