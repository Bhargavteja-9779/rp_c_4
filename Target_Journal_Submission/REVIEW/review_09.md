# Review round 9 — Associate Editor assessment (Analytica Chimica Acta)

Role: Associate Editor deciding whether to send the manuscript to external review, and predicting its fate there. I do not act as an author.

## Assessment
| Criterion | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Scope** | Fits: chemometric methodology (uncertainty of transferred multivariate calibrations) with direct relevance to analytical practice. The calibration-transfer and multivariate-uncertainty literature cited includes several ACA papers. |
| **Novelty** | Moderate but genuine. The authors state honestly that jackknife+ is not theirs. What is new is the problem framing (interval validity after transfer), the instantiation (re-fitting only the transfer), the evidence that common practice fails, and practical rules. A reviewer focused on theory may call it incremental; the empirical breadth and the honesty about competitors mitigate this. |
| **Technical quality** | High. Pre-registered design, a deviation log, paired tests, multiple data sets, an independent post-review test (E8) and a competitor test (E9). One implementation error, found during internal review (LV sensitivity), was fixed and disclosed. |
| **Evidence vs claims** | Claims are proportionate. The abstract claims validity, not superiority in efficiency, and discloses Gaussian competitiveness and the drift failure. |
| **Completeness** | Complete for a methods paper. No industrial or process case study; acknowledged. |
| **Reproducibility** | Excellent: public data with checksums, one-command reproduction, chunked execution, all numbers generated from result files. |
| **Clarity** | Good after revision. The manuscript is long (≈8,500 words in the body, 7 tables, 9 figures, 43 manuscript pages). |
| **Compliance** | Structured abstract ≤ 250 words, highlights, graphical abstract, keywords, declarations (CRediT, competing interests, generative AI, data availability) and Elsevier numeric references are all present. Author details are placeholders, to be completed before submission. |
| **Reviewer risk** | Main risks: (i) "incremental relative to jackknife+"; (ii) "Gaussian intervals are nearly as good, so why bother"; (iii) length. |

## Required editorial changes (applied in this round)
1. **Reduce main-text load.** The robustness table (21 rows) duplicates Fig. 7 and has moved to the Supplementary Material (Table S9). The timing figure has moved to the Supplementary Material (Fig. S3), because its message fits in one sentence. Main text now has 7 tables and 9 figures.
2. **Numbering audit after the moves.** Tables 1–7 and Figs. 1–9 now appear in order of first citation. Supplementary items S1–S9 and Figs. S1–S3 all exist and are cited.
3. **Risk (ii) must be met head-on.** It is, in §4.3 (Gauss-LOO, α-dependence; Gauss-LOO-N ≈3% more efficient but invalid in 14/192 scenarios) and in the Limitations. Keep.

- **Author information, funding, repository URL and archived DOI** must be completed before submission.

Likely to receive a **major revision** decision, with a reasonable chance of acceptance after revision, provided the reviewers accept a methodological-application contribution. Desk-rejection risk is moderate (scope is fine; the novelty framing is the main risk). No fatal flaw identified.
